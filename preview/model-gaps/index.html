    Part 3 · How I'd build the gaps

    1. Two stages, every time

    Low-poly parts first, so the whole town is populated and playable. Then the upgrade pass on what players actually look at: hero buildings, the people near the camera, the animals you can pet. You're right that this is the best process: it ends up with something in the world on day one, and each upgrade replaces a model without touching the rest.

    2. Scene-Generator-style Blender code is the main builder

    Most of the list (0 items marked S) is simple engineered shapes: hydrants, poles, signs, awnings, fences, houses, shopfronts, small birds. These are best written as a short Blender Python recipe, the way the ChatGPT scene generator did it, then exported as a named-parts GLB. Each recipe lives with its brief in the asset folder, so a change request makes a new version.

    3. Free kits where they exist

    0 items marked P: farm animals, vehicles, trees and holiday props already have good CC0 / CC-BY models. Check the licence, downsample in the Blender pipeline, record the source in provenance.json. Nothing BY-SA, NC or personal-use.

    4. Variants of what we already have

    0 items marked V are a re-dress of an existing character or animal (new hat, new tool, new colour). Cheapest of all, and they keep the family look.

    5. Meshy only for hero animals and the few organic shapes

    Meshy untextured plus Blender cleanup stays for the things code can't shape well: the deer, the fox, the heron. About 20 credits each, so 10–15 animals is a sensible first batch.

    Saturday: the drone and photo inventory

    What to capture

    1. A straight-down drone pass over the common, Main Street, the school and Leonard Field, so rooftops and street layout match the real town.
    2. One low front-on photo of every shopfront on Main Street (sign blank in our version, so only the look is used).
    3. Three photos of each house type: colonial, cape, victorian, triple-decker, with the door, porch and window trim close up.
    4. The small stuff, one photo each: hydrant, mailbox, utility pole, street sign, bench, bike rack, storm drain, stone wall.
    5. The things that make it Winchester: the mill pond spillway, the train station, the town clock, the church steeple, the playground, the skate park.
    6. Animals you actually see: write down which ones and where. That list is better than mine.

    No house numbers, no faces, no family addresses in anything we publish. Photos stay private; only the shapes and colours we extract go in the game.

    How the photos become the diorama

    Drone pass sets the layout and heights. Each photo becomes a brief for a Blender recipe, built low-poly first, then upgraded. Each model is shown next to its source photo in the viewer, like the modeler vision asks.
