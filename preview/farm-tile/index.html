The Seed Beds

A farm tile where the cards in your hand are seed packets, and every packet is a colour. Throw one at a bed and the garden answers. Proposal only: nothing is built until Evan picks.

Try the idea

Pick a packet, tap a bed, then slide the day clock. This is a stand-in sketch (flat shapes, not final art) so you can feel the pacing: a sprout in seconds, a bloom in a minute, bees when two colours neighbour.

The soul

I threw a little colour, and the ground said thank you. This is Paint the World's feeling, slowed down and made kind. In Paint the World the sky throws paint and strange plants answer. Here you throw: the seed card falls like a paint drop, squashes into the bed with the same overshoot, and the colour you threw becomes the colour of what grows.

The Ranch speaks in wood, bells and painted signs, so the farm does too: each bed has a small painted marker that shows its packet's splat, a bell on a post rings once at harvest, and Bub trots over to whichever bed is thirsty. No bars, no numbers.

How it plays

1. Pull the tileThe Farm comes off the shelf like any tile (rolls like a die, then a reveal). For the reveal I suggest the brick-rain one (E): soil, beds and fence posts tumble in and settle. Seven raised beds in a hex: one in the middle, six around it, the same shape as Winchester.
2. Play a packetHold a seed card, drag it over a bed, let go. It splats in with the paint-drop recipe. Tap a card first and it opens as a held card; its back shows what it likes (sun, rain, neighbours) as little pictures, no words.
3. Watch it growGrowth is driven by the one day clock, like Paint the World, so pausing pauses it. Sprout in seconds, grow, bloom. A bed that wants rain droops; a scarecrow leans. Bees and butterflies arrive when a plant is ready to make a seed.
4. NeighboursDorfromantik's idea, kindly: two beds that touch matter. Complementary colours (yellow next to blue) attract more bees, and bees leave a new mixed seed in the bed between. That is how you discover cards. Nothing can fail; a bad pairing is just a plainer garden.
5. HarvestWhen the bell rings, tap the bed. The plant pops into a harvest card (Sun stage) in your hand. Harvest cards feed the café: Paint the World already says plants should become café ingredients.
6. Come back tomorrowThe farm keeps growing while you're away, from the saved day count, so there is always something to find. (Animal Crossing's lesson: always one more small thing.)

Cards

The cards are Cards v3 die-cuts: the bed stands up out of the card. Rarity is already Seed, Sprout, Bloom, Harvest, Sun in the Card Forge, so a seed card literally grows through its own rarity ladder as you use it. The first Ranch set is five seed packets and two helpers:

Assumptions table (first principles)

Per the first-principles rule, growth comes from a small visible table, not hand-placed lore. Numbers are proposals to tune in play.

QuantityProposed
Sprout after planting0.5 game hours (about 15 real seconds at default speed)
Full growth20 game hours, like Paint the World's plants; Pumpkin 36
Water needRain once per game day; a dry bed grows at half speed, never dies
Beds7 per tile, 1 plant family each
Bee pair bonusTouching complementary colours: +1 mixed seed per bloom
Caps14 plants visible per bed pair, pooled meshes, one shared shader (no hitch)

Rewards

Backpack charms only, no coins. The charm set is fixed at ten, so a farm charm is a real decision for Evan: I recommend one new charm, a sunflower, earned when a whole tile of beds is in bloom at once ("a field facing the same way"). If you'd rather keep ten, the farm could instead pay the existing heart or add the café cup's ingredients with no charm. The harvest itself is the everyday reward: new cards.

Why this has an identity

Stardew's farm is about work and routine. Ours is about colour you throw, and a garden that answers. It is the Ranch's cousin of Paint the World, it uses the card ladder we already ship, and it feeds the café and the Moon Mission (a bed on the Moon is a good late surprise). Tiny Glade and Dorfromantik set the taste: calm, tactile, nothing you can lose.

Tour: yes. One verb (throw a seed), a payoff within seconds. The riding school is no for now.
