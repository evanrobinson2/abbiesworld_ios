Trick Day

A riding school tile where you teach a horse tricks by playing cards, and you can watch it get better. Gentle, silly and proud, made for a six or seven year old. Dressage is the grown-up version of the same game. Proposal only: nothing is built until Evan picks.

The soul

A horse learns because you're kind, and you can see it learning. No whip, no failing. The horse can say "not today" (it turns its tail and munches grass), and that is fine. Modern horse trainers teach exactly this way: reward the tiny right thing and the horse offers more, which is called positive-reinforcement or clicker training. That is our whole teaching loop, and it is the same loop as our practice reps: attempt, a little better, retry, and you can see the progression.

The Ranch dialect again: wood, bells and painted signs. A brass bell on the arena gate is the "good!" sound. Each stall has a painted nameplate. And, echoing Paint the World, hoofprints leave coloured paint on the sand, so a routine draws a picture, and the picture is how you can tell it's good.

How it plays

1. Pull the tileThe School comes off the shelf, rolls like a die, and the fence rails hammer in around a sand ring with a little stable, a hay bale, a mounting block and a bell gate.
2. Meet a horseOpen a stall. Three horses at first, each with a different shy or silly streak (one loves to bow, one is a show-off, one is nervous of poles). Give a pat or a carrot card and it comes to you; the nervous one needs more.
3. Play a trick cardDrag a trick card onto the ring. The horse tries it. The first try is wobbly and funny (the bow is a nod, the spin is half a spin). Ring the bell for a good one and it tries again, a bit better.
4. It remembersA well-learned trick shows on its card (the picture upgrades from Seed to Sun, our rarity ladder). Later the horse does it by itself when you walk past, because it wants to. That is the pay-off feeling.
5. Build a routineLine up three trick cards, like a dance phrase. The horse does them in order to the ring's music, tied to the beat as in Just Dance. Same cards, new order, new routine.
6. Show DayFriends come and sit on the bleachers; the horse does its routine and a rosette ribbon hangs on its stall. This is where Abbie, Ani and Bub can cheer.

Cards

Same Cards v3 die-cut as the farm: the horse steps out of the card. Tricks are chosen to be big, readable and giggly at six or seven:

TrickWhat you see
BowFront knees down, head low, like a curtsy
SpinA slow twirl on the spot
Yes (nod)The horse nods to a question
WaveOne front hoof lifted, pawing the air
WeaveSnakes between painted cones
HopA small hop over a low pole
SidestepSteps sideways along the fence
StatueStands completely still while a bird lands on it

Helper cards: Pat, Carrot (a treat only), Brush, Bell. Nothing scary: no rearing, no falling off, no whips. Rarity runs Seed (wobbly) to Sun (shows off).

Dressage, the grown-up version

Dressage is too abstract for six year olds, but it is a good ladder for older kids and grown-ups. Same engine: in Patterns mode you draw a shape on the sand with your finger (a circle, a figure eight, a serpentine) and the horse walks it, hoofprints painting the line. The score is how true the paint is. The difference between the two games is only the view: a child sees a proud horse; a grown-up sees the lines and marks. That is the two-view idea from the information grammar.

Rewards

Backpack charms only, no coins. Charms are fixed at ten, so this is Evan's call: I recommend one new charm, a horseshoe, for a first Show Day routine. The everyday reward is the horse itself learning, plus rosettes on the stall door (decoration in the world, not currency).

Why this has an identity

Other horse games (Star Stable, Spirit Riding Free) are about riding and adventure. Ours is about teaching and trust: you are the coach, the horse is the star. It comes from the Ranch's own love of animals and from the practice-reps idea, with Paint the World's colour trail so the horse's work is a picture. Red Dead 2's horse bond is the high bar for "the horse remembers you"; we borrow the feeling, not the realism.
