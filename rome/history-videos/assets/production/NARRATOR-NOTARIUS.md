# The Notarius — narrator for the whole piece

Status: **DRAFT for Evan's review**
Supersedes the handoff structure in the first draft of `OPENING-NOTARIUS.md`.

---

## The idea

A Roman *notarius* — the clerk who counted the Goths at the river — narrates the entire
story. He does not hand off to a Gothic voice. He **converts.**

The film is a deposition he should have taken thirty years ago.

## The pronoun is the arc

This is the whole device. The narrator's moral movement is carried by which word he uses for
whom, and the audience feels it before they can name it.

| Section | Pronoun | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Cold open | **I** | The clerk alone with the tablet. Complicit, precise, wrong. |
| Act 1 — the North | **they** | The Goths, observed from outside. Bureaucratic distance. He is reconstructing what he never saw. |
| Act 2 — crossing, hunger | **we** = *Rome* | The confession. "We counted them. We priced them." |
| Act 2 — banquet | **we** = *Rome*, fooled | "We thought we had surprised them." He records what Rome believed. |
| Act 3 — Adrianople | the flip | The cavalry arrives. His allegiance breaks in the middle of the battle. |
| Close | **they**, then **I** | Same word as Act 1, opposite weight — accounting becomes awe. Then back to "I" for the correction. |

"They" at the start is a tally. "They will not beg again" at the end is the same pronoun said
with reverence. That bookend costs nothing and does everything.

## The confession reframe

The existing humiliation lyric is in Gothic first person:

> They counted us. / They priced us. / They took the iron we had carried for generations.

In the notarius's mouth it inverts into an admission:

> We counted them. / We priced them. / We took the iron they had carried for generations.

Same lines, opposite speaker, and now somebody is accountable for them. This is stronger
than the original and it costs one pronoun per line.

## How the Gothic voice survives — quotation

The best writing from the 2026-08-25 session is Gothic first person and must not be lost.
Solution: the notarius **quotes** them. Transcribing testimony is literally his office, so
quotation is in character rather than a workaround.

> They said: *we came to you in peace.*
> They said: *we asked with empty hands.*

The Alaric passage stays untouched, because it is already third person — which now reads as
the notarius finally recording the thing he omitted:

> Alaric was a child when this happened.
> He saw his dad kneel before some man.
> ...
> They crossed the river. They begged for bread.
> They will not beg again.

Nothing in that passage needs rewriting. It was always the notarius's voice; we just didn't
know who was speaking yet.

## Why this fits what is already built

- **Principle 5** — Romans as antagonists by procedure, not caricature. Here procedure
  becomes a person and then changes his mind. That is the principle dramatized rather than
  merely obeyed.
- **Open Choice #1** in `SONG-ARC-STANZAS-02-07.md` — "same elder throughout, or collective
  'we' that becomes 'they' for Rome?" Answered, and better than either option: one speaker
  whose pronouns migrate.
- **The reversal** needs someone who was fooled in good faith. He writes down what Rome
  believed, so when the staged betrayal is revealed he is wrong alongside the audience. "The
  Caesar is the one that looks surprised" — so is the man with the tablet.
- **`bible/06-uncertainty-ledger.md`** grades every claim from attested to invented. The
  narrator can say out loud where he is guessing, which makes the QR-code citation mechanism
  a character trait instead of production apparatus bolted to the frame.
- **The two-tribe withholding.** `bible/01-creative-principles.md` already forbids cutting to
  the Gothic cavalry so the audience knows where it is. A Roman narrator *cannot* know it is
  coming. The withholding stops being a rule we impose and becomes a fact of who is talking.

That last one is the strongest structural payoff. The cavalry arriving over the ridge is a
surprise *to the narrator*, which is why it can be a surprise to the audience.

## Draft — cold open

Register **R2 ink-wash**, observational. Runs over bass and kick only, no full kit.

```
I was the one who counted them.

Not the man who fed them. Not the man who took their iron.
I held the tablet. I wrote the number down.

The number was wrong. There were more.
There are always more than the number.

What follows is not the record.
The record is thin — a river, a famine, a battle, an emperor gone.
What follows is what we made of it.

Where I am certain, I have said so.
Where I am guessing, the mark is at the corner of the frame.
Check me. I would rather be checked than believed.

They came to the water in the year the steppe moved.
I wrote: permitted to cross.
I wrote: iron surrendered.
I wrote: bread issued, in part.

I did not write down what the boy saw.

That is the part I got wrong.
```

## Draft — the turn at Adrianople

The moment his allegiance breaks. Third act, as the Greuthungi cavalry arrives.

```
We had them. That is what I wrote, later, when my hand was steady.
We had them on the field and the day was ours.

I did not write the sound.
I have no word for the sound the ridge made.

They came from the east, and they were not the tribe we had counted.
They were the other one. The one we turned away.
The one nobody wrote down.
```

"The one nobody wrote down" ties the second tribe to his own failure of record. The cavalry
is not a *deus ex machina* — it is the consequence of a document that left people out.

## Open

1. **Does he name himself?** Recommend no. An unnamed clerk is procedure; a named one is a
   character competing with Fritigern and Alaric for the audience's attachment.
2. **Delivery.** Evan's voice throughout is now simpler, since there is only one speaker.
   Quoted Gothic lines could shift in tone rather than in performer.
3. **Does he survive to 410?** If the coda reaches the Sack of Rome, he would be very old or
   dead. Options: he writes it as an old man, or the final section is the record continuing
   without him — which is bleaker and possibly better.
4. **Fritigern's youth in Act 1** is the weak point of this frame. A Roman clerk has no
   plausible access to a Gothic boyhood on the steppe. Either he admits he is inventing it
   (which the "where I am guessing" line licenses), or Act 1 is the one section he does not
   narrate.

Item 4 is the real problem to solve. Everything else is settled.
