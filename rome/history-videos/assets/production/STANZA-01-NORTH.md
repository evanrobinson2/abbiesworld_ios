## 2. Core visual concept — *Goths shadowing horse-ridden conquistadors*

The Huns are **not cast as characters**. They are a **mounted silhouette language** that invades otherwise pastoral frames.

| Layer | What the viewer sees |
| --- | --- |
| **Gothic world (foreground)** | Wagons, timber halls, pasture, women arguing a goat back, bread in a wagon shelter, children playing between wheel spokes |
| **Hun presence (background / edge)** | 3–7 **elongated horse+rider shadows** on a ridgeline — cut-paper indigo silhouettes, no faces, no detail, dust gold wedge behind |
| **The "shadowing" turn** | Late in the stanza: a **Gothic scout on a nervous horse** holds the **same silhouette pose** as the ridge riders — not imitation of heroism, but **mimicry of survival**; the conquered shape entering the body of the threatened people |

Think **conquistador** as grammar, not costume: sudden horizon ownership, entitlement to movement across land, terror from height and speed. The Goths do not become Huns — they **inherit the shadow** of mounted power because the steppe has arrived.

**Bible compliance:** Mood 1 Dread — no visible antagonist. Riders on the ridge are **shape only**, never a named Hun, never a clear face.

## 4. Lyric direction (draft — dubbing-safe)

Spoken, rhythmic, short clauses. Bass carries harmony; voice is top line. No idioms.

```
We had the north.
Wood smoke. Wet grass. A wheel that still turned.

Sometimes a knife —
a boundary, a debt, a grazing field —
and then the knife went back in its sheath.

Children learned the wagon.
Elders learned the weather.
We thought the arguments were the hard part.

Then the ridge line wore a new shape.
Horse. Rider. Horse. Rider.
Not a battle yet.
A shadow learning how to ride.

We did not see their faces.
We saw what they did to the horizon.
```

**Interlude hook** (not this stanza): *"This is not the end"* — reserve for later.

Tone: weary memory, not prophecy. Speaker knows what comes; stanza 1 should still **feel** like safety.
