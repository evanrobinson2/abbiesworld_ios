# Timeline continuity — reconciling the Hunnic wedding attack

Status: **proposed resolution, needs Evan's yes**
Companion to `mind/STORY_ARC.md`. Resolves the one collision in that document.

---

## The collision

`mind/STORY_ARC.md` places the wedding attack (Part I) and Alaric's birth (Part II) before
the migration (Part III). Evan has confirmed the attackers are **Huns**.

But the lyric and the handoff both require Alaric to be roughly **eight when Valens dies**
(378), old enough to witness the humiliation and to have "his best friend stolen." That puts
his birth around **370**.

If the Hunnic attack on the wedding is the same event as the Hunnic collapse of Gothic life
(375–376), then Alaric is born around 375 and is **two or three** at Adrianople. A toddler
cannot witness, and cannot lose a best friend.

## The resolution — the Huns arrived over years, not in one night

This is not a workaround. The Hunnic advance was genuinely gradual:

| Date | Event |
| --- | --- |
| c. 370 | Huns break the **Alans** on the Don. Raiding reaches the Gothic east. |
| c. 375–376 | Huns destroy the **Greuthungi**; their king Ermanaric dies, reportedly by his own hand. |
| 376 | Pressure reaches the **Thervingi**. Athanaric's resistance fails. Fritigern and Alavivus petition Valens. |
| 376 | Danube crossing. Marcianople. |
| 9 Aug 378 | Adrianople. Valens dies. |

So there is a **five-to-six year escalation**, not a single blow. That gives us:

- **c. 370 — the wedding, attacked by a Hunnic raiding party.** The first touch. Terrifying,
  local, survivable. The elders die.
- **c. 370–371 — Alaric is born.** Child of the union, as Part II requires.
- **c. 375–376 — life becomes impossible.** The raids that began at the wedding have become
  a wall moving west. Now they leave.
- **378 — Alaric is eight.** Old enough to witness everything, and to remember it for thirty
  years.

## Why this is thematically better than a single attack

The wedding raid is not the cause of the migration. It is the **first warning nobody heeded.**
Six years of deteriorating life sit between the wound and the flight.

That is how displacement actually works. You do not leave the first time. You leave when you
can no longer stay. Fritigern spends six years trying to keep a life that is already ending —
which is exactly the "practical, rooted, reluctant" man Part I describes.

It also gives the Huns the right dramatic treatment per `assets/production/STANZA-01-NORTH.md`:
never characters, only "a mounted silhouette language." A raiding party at a wedding, then
shapes on a ridge for six years, then a wall. Dread accumulating, per mood 1 — *"a threat never
clearly seen."*

## Where the two peoples separate — at the river, not on the steppe

Evan: *"to save the tribes, they went separate ways."*

Recommend placing that split at the **Danube in 376**, because history already did it:

- The **Thervingi** (Fritigern, Alavivus) petitioned Valens and were **admitted**.
- The **Greuthungi** (Alatheus, Saphrax) were **refused** and crossed later without permission.

So the separation is not a tactical choice made in the wild. It is **Rome dividing a family at
a border**. One half is let in and processed, counted, priced, disarmed. The other half is
turned away and vanishes.

Three beats, each historically anchored:

1. **The Huns drive them together** — the wedding, the union, the flight.
2. **Rome divides them** — the river. Admission for some, refusal for the rest.
3. **Adrianople reunites them** — the horsemen return and the Roman army is destroyed.

The steppe horsemen do not merely recede so the audience forgets. **The empire removes them.**
Which makes their return an indictment rather than a coincidence: Rome split this family, and
the family came back together to break Rome's army.

## Consequence — Alaric's mother

If Alaric is with Fritigern through the humiliation, his mother is too. So she is the woman who
married across the divide and then lost her own people at the river.

She should be in the recognition frame at Adrianople. The beat is not only *Alaric sees his
mother's people* — it is **the mother sees her people**, after two years believing them gone.
Alaric learns what his inheritance is by watching his mother's face.

That gives the cavalry arrival a human close-up instead of only spectacle, and it gives her a
function beyond being the bride in Part I.
