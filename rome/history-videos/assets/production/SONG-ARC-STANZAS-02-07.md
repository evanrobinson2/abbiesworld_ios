# Stanza 2 — The Walk

**Placement:** ~35–45s after Stanza 1  
**Historical beat:** Decision to move; column of families, oxen, wagons; weeks of road; weather; wheel breaks and is repaired (continuity).

## What it must do

- Earn Uprooting — the north is **behind** them now
- Show the walk as **physical fact**: mud, rain, lame ox, argument over direction
- Keep Principle 4: someone still laughs on the road
- Horse-shadow grammar may **reappear on distant ridges** — dread without pursuit battle

## Mood mix

| Mood | Weight |
| --- | --- |
| Uprooting (2) | 50% |
| Endurance (4) | 30% |
| Tenderness (7) | 10% |
| Dread (1) | 10% |

## Visual concept

The column is a **living serpent** of cut-paper bands: wagons, silhouettes, livestock. Camera alternates **enormous** (whole column in a valley) and **intimate** (stone in sandal, child's hand on wagon rail). Looking **back** is a recurring gesture — north is smoke on horizon, not a map.

## Lyric sketch

```
We walked.
Not heroically — we walked because the ridge did not empty.

Wheels sank. Wheels rose.
Someone mended what broke.
Someone cursed what would not mend.

The children slept upright.
The old were carried when they could not walk.
We told stories so the road would end.

It did not end.
It bent south.
```

# Stanza 3 — The Crossing

**Placement:** ~40–50s  
**Historical beat:** Petition to Valens's officials; disarmament; hostages; hunger; Roman boats control the river.

## What it must do

- **Humiliation is the hinge** — audience must believe the revolt later
- Romans antagonists by **procedure**, not caricature (Principle 5)
- **Seed Alaric:** child silhouette in corral line (not named on screen)
- Petition: Fritigern and Alavivus **bowed**, empty hands

## Mood mix

| Mood | Weight |
| --- | --- |
| Humiliation (5) | 40% |
| Petition (3) | 20% |
| Hunger (6) | 20% |
| Endurance (4) | 20% |

## Visual concept

**Compression.** Horizon rises; layers **reduce**. Wide north opens into **corralled bands**. Roman authority is **vertical** (platforms, poles, boats on water). Gothic bodies **horizontal** — waiting, kneeling, reaching.

## Lyric sketch

```
The river was a line.
Rome made it a gate.

They counted us.
They priced us.
They took the iron we had carried for generations.

We asked permission to live.
We asked with empty hands.

A child watched from the rope lane —
too young to know this was history.
Old enough to remember the taste of waiting.

Bread came late.
Bread came with a hand that did not let go.
```

# Stanza 4 — The Banquet

**Placement:** ~45–55s  
**Historical beat:** Marcianople invitation; conviviality; skirmish news; betrayal; murders inside room (R3); fury at gate.

## What it must do

- **R1 → R3 → R1** grammar lesson: we **cannot** go inside the killing
- Lupicinus **genuinely warm** before turn (Principle 5)
- Geography: courtyard → door → gate (Principle 6)
- Alavivus **vanishes** — last appearance half in shadow

## Mood mix

| Mood | Weight |
| --- | --- |
| Levity (8) | 15% |
| Unease (9) | 25% |
| Betrayal (10) | 35% |
| Fury (11) | 25% |

## Visual concept

**Information has geography.** We stay with who **does not know**. Messenger runs **through** courtyard past retainers. Banquet warmth **compresses** into door-slice light, then **charcoal void**, then **courtyard burst** and **gate fury**.

## Lyric sketch

```
They called it peace.
They set a table.

Cups went up.
Words went down easy.
The courtyard still held our men — fifteen paces from the door.

Then running feet.
Then a face that had seen blood.
Then a door that learned to close.

We do not know what the room did.
We know what the city did after.
```

# Stanza 5 — The Wagon Fight

**Placement:** ~40–50s  
**Historical beat:** Laager battles across Thrace; wagon circle as field fort; women passing arrows; breach and cost.

## What it must do

- **Real battle** — not symbolic (user direction)
- Fury ≠ Terror: **chosen** violence, offensive defense
- Show **cost** — breached laager aftermath
- Young warrior with **Roman shield** acquired (continuity seed → Adrianople)

## Mood mix

| Mood | Weight |
| --- | --- |
| Fury (11) | 85% |
| Ruin (15) | 15% (aftermath only) |

## Visual concept

**Circle grammar.** Wagons = walls. Every frame either **inside** the ring (intimacy, families) or **outside** (Roman mass). Aerial → ground → night fire → **broken circle** end.

## Lyric sketch

```
When the road could not hold us, we made a wall of what we had.
Wheels. Boards. Bodies.

They came for the gap.
We came for the gap first.

Women handed arrows like bread.
Children learned a new silence.

The circle broke somewhere.
It always breaks somewhere.
We carried what we could.
```

# Stanza 6 — Years of Rebellion → Alaric

**Placement:** ~50–60s (montage stanza — most time-compressed)  
**Historical span:** 378 Adrianople → decades of raiding → Alaric king → first siege 408.

## What it must do

- **Time jump** without losing thread: same cut-paper world, **worsening wear** on objects
- Adrianople = **Terror** (suffered), not Fury
- Valens arc: Hubris → Terror → Ruin (empty horse)
- **Alaric reveal:** child from stanza 3 → young commander → king at horse head
- Raiding = **Procedure** on Roman land + **Fury** on soldiers — not random cruelty porn

## Mood mix

| Mood | Weight |
| --- | --- |
| Terror (14) | 35% |
| Ruin (15) | 25% |
| Fury (11) | 20% |
| Hubris (13) | 10% |
| Endurance (4) | 10% |

## Visual concept

**Montage via wear.** Same wagon wheel **more damaged** each beat. Same shield **more battered**. Maps/tablets ignored (Valens). Dust → cavalry → empty field → **years pass as paper yellowing** (subtle bone shift). Final beat: **Alaric** stylized face, mature, not sanctified — intelligent, tired, determined.

**Register:** Mostly R1; one **R2** beat for "years of raiding" (observational wider frames).

## Lyric sketch

```
Years are not a song.
They are a road that keeps receipt.

Heat at Adrianople.
An emperor's horse without a rider.
Fields we did not plant, we still walked through.

Raid. Treaty. Raid again.
Rome learned our name in pieces.

The child at the rope lane grew tall.
They called him king.
He did not ask to be a king.
He asked Rome to keep a promise.

Rome answered with walls.
```

# Stanza 7 — The Sack of Rome

**Placement:** ~45–55s — **finale**  
**Historical beat:** 24 August 410 — Salarian Gate; three days; Alaric dies soon after (may epilogue as Ruin coda).

## What it must do

- **Ruin at civilizational scale** — not looting montage
- Principle 7: frame ethics — civilian POV, smoke through streets, **not** rape/pillage spectacle
- Goths are **avengers and destroyers** — same paper dignity, no triumph music in image
- Gold = **fire only** — burning layered paper Rome skyline
- End on **stillness**, not glory

## Mood mix

| Mood | Weight |
| --- | --- |
| Ruin (15) | 70% |
| Terror (14) | 20% |
| Fury (11) | 10% |

## Visual concept

Rome as **stacked cut-paper monuments** — simplified late-antique silhouettes (not medieval towers). Smoke gold wedges. Gothic silhouettes **entering** through gate breach **small against city scale**. Alaric on horse **small in frame** — history is bigger than him. Final image: **empty street**, fallen laurel wreath as **object not emblem**, paper dissolving to bone.

**No text.** No "ROME" letters. No SPQR.

## Lyric sketch

```
The city was paper before we touched it.
Layers. Laws. Altars. Markets.
Rome had always been a story told about itself.

We did not come to admire the story.
We came because the river gate stayed closed.

Smoke learned the streets.
Gold learned the night — not wealth, fire.

When it was over, the silence was older than the trumpets.
An empire does not die in a day.
It learns a new shape.
```
