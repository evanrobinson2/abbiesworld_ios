# Tiny Aurora — functional design baseline

Accepted as the working design direction by Evan on 2026-10-04.
Owner: 003 — Ship Designer.

## Design intent

A compact deep-space expedition ship with ten functional bays connected by
corridors. Creator (004) develops the aesthetic; Designer (003) develops the
functional organization and design intent; Architect (002) translates that into
a granular physical specification; Builder (001) realizes the dioramas.

Six crew and three decks are working assumptions, not frozen requirements.
Gravity remains unresolved, so deck orientation and hull proportions remain
provisional. The room schedule and connections can develop before those settle.

## Bay schedule

| Proposed area | Bay | Purpose |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Command | 01 Flight deck | Piloting, navigation and communications |
| Living | 02 Captain's quarters | One berth, desk and personal storage |
| Living | 03 Officers' quarters | Two berths, shared desk and individual storage |
| Living | 04 Crew quarters | Three berths, privacy and individual storage |
| Living | 05 Galley / mess | Food preparation, communal meals and off-duty gathering |
| Living | 06 Hygiene / medical suite | Internally separated hygiene facilities and treatment space |
| Service | 07 Life support | Accessible air processing, water recovery and replaceable equipment |
| Service | 08 Cargo / workshop | Secured stores, spares, tools and repair workspace |
| Service | 09 Airlock / suit preparation | Exterior transition, suit preparation and equipment stowage |
| Service | 10 Engineering | Power/propulsion monitoring and machinery service access |

Bays are functional compartments of different sizes, not ten identical structural
modules. Corridors and the vertical connection are additional space. Tanks,
propulsion machinery, structure, utility routes and maintenance clearances also
need volume outside this room count. This is a design baseline, not an engineering
validation of deep-space endurance or safety.

## Circulation and adjacency

- Cabins open onto a quiet shared passage, with the mess at its communal end.
  No cabin or workroom is a required through route to another bay.
- A service corridor links airlock, cargo/workshop and engineering. Moving cargo
  and repair equipment does not require passing through living spaces.
- Life support opens onto the service corridor for maintenance access.
- A common vertical connection links command, living and service areas.
- Door positions, passage clearances, vertical transport, emergency routes and
  utility routing must be resolved in the Architect's physical specification.

## First coherent three-room slice

Develop cargo/workshop, life support and engineering together with their shared
service corridor. Show the corridor's airlock connection as a continuation beyond
the slice. Preserve this connectivity in individual room dioramas.

The three rooms should read as distinct functions: secured stores and a repair
bench; serviceable processing equipment; and engineering controls with machinery
access. Equipment placement must preserve circulation and maintenance access.
Exact dimensions, object construction, camera geometry and render acceptance
criteria are the next architectural handoff, not established by this baseline.

## Aesthetic handoff

Creator's latest proposal describes a maintained industrial expedition vessel,
with warmer inhabited spaces inside a cooler pale hull. That remains aesthetic
input under Creator's ownership. Palette, motifs, surface treatments and final
exterior silhouette are not frozen here.
