Changing worlds

A world is a whole board. People need to switch which board is shown, start a new one, name it and keep them all. Pull back and your boards are hexes of a bigger board; tap one and you land in it.

Switching boards

Your boards, and a way to make a new one

Today the sandbox has one saved board. Boards are already stored under their own ids (card-library.js), and a single "current" pointer says which one is shown, so more than one board needs a library, a pointer move and a way to see them. The land is a separate choice: ?world=basin swaps the floor, and a board can stand on either. The prototype shows three ways to switch. Tap a tile on the board to place one, then switch away and back: nothing is lost.

What just happened, by information mode
    A · Shelf

    Boards on a shelf

    The board sits on a table. A shelf at the edge holds miniatures of your other boards and an empty frame. Tap a miniature and the table slides to that board.

    • Diegetic and instantly understood: boards are objects.
    • Fits three or four boards; ten crowd the shelf.
    • A frame on the shelf is the new-board button.
    B · Recommended

    Pull back to the board of boards

    Pinch or scroll out past the edge of your board and every board is one hex of a bigger board, each with its painted name. A ghost hex beside them is the next new board. Tap a hex and the camera lands in it.

    • No menu: it is the same camera and the same hex grammar as the tiles, and the same idea as Winchester's seven hexes.
    • Scales from two boards to thirty; the map never reshuffles, each board keeps its slot.
    • The new board is where you can see it will go.
    C · World menu

    A Boards list

    The existing ◎ World menu gets a Boards page: thumbnail, name, land, tile count, and a New row. It is how most apps do it.

    • Precise and quick to build, and good for a grown-up's rename and clean-up.
    • An overlay: it leaves the world and shows rows, not places.
    • Keep it as the grown-up page behind B, not the first thing a child meets.
    Start a new boardThree ways, all from the empty hex or the New button: a blank clearing, a copy of the board you are on (the original never changes, and the copy remembers where it came from), or a blank board on another land such as the Basin. A new board is shown at once, ready for the first tile.
    Name itThe name lives on a painted wooden sign over the board, in the Ranch's materials. Tap the sign to type. Names are trimmed to 32 letters and unique; a new board starts as "A place of your own".
    Keep itEvery change saves on its own, as today. Switching only moves the "current" pointer, so nothing is overwritten and every board keeps its own undo history.
    Put it awayA board is never deleted by one tap. It goes to the attic and Bring one back returns it. You can never put your last board away.

    The model is simulations/worlds/boards.mjs (new, copy, rename, place, attic, the stable board map) with tests. Mapping to the game: the library is a list of the board ids already in the boards store; pointerOf(library) is the existing current pointer. Tour: yes, once there are two boards: "Pinch out to see all your worlds."

      Three options

      Doorways, tiles, or a map

      A · Recommended

      Doorways and rides

      Every world has a physical way out and the same thing is the way back: the black truck, the train at Ginn Field, the ranch gate, the pink rocket on its port. Rooms (cellar, school, lab) are plain doors.

      • Diegetic: the characters know how to leave. Sound is the cue (engine, bell, horn).
      • Spatial: a soft ring over the way, only near it.
      • One rule teaches a six-year-old: go the way you came.
      • Cost: every world needs a ride, and the first ride is story, so it needs writing.
      B · Hex tile

      Drop the tile

      The way is a glowing hex socket. You hold a world tile from the backpack and drop it there: it falls, the world rises out of it, and you step in. This is the tile-drop reveal and the pink rocket tile.

      • Lovely for a new world: it is a gift, and a card to collect.
      • Slower for the tenth trip; a tile is an object, not a habit.
      • Fits Winchester's seven hexes: a region is a tile on the table.
      • Cost: you must hold the right tile; decoys need a gentle "does not fit".
      C · Backpack map

      Pins on a glass map

      A map page in the backpack. Only places you have been are pinned. Tap one: the camera lifts, flies and lands at the way back.

      • Fastest and clearest for a grown-up and for "take me home".
      • Weakest on the canon: it is overlay, and every place becomes a menu row.
      • Works well as a reward (earned pins) and as a Daddy's-memories surface.
      • Cost: it skips the arrival feel unless the landing is staged.

      The recommendation, spelled out

      Doors for rooms, rides for worlds, and two small helpers

      Leave and arriveLeave by the thing you came on or the door you opened. Arrive stepping off the same thing on the other side, with a pin over the way back. The camera goes through the way and comes out zoomed on the other side, in about a second and a half. A ride that crosses worlds plays a short calm cutscene the first time and can be skipped after.
      The backpack follows youOne store: coins, items (café key, rocket tile, moon key) and the visited list. First visits pay out once (basement ten coins, school roof one, the Moon base gives its key). The cellar stays shut and rattles until the key is in the backpack: the world simply behaves.
      Core-memory placesHold up a gold coin and remember: it warms, hums, and the place builds itself round the family. It only works for places already visited, and it is a backpack page, not a menu. The family arrives together, at the way back.
      The family TVWhen the whole crew changes world, everyone is aboard the same ride. Each presses their job (Oxygen, Lights and Radio, Commander). A fork is a vote with chips; a tie goes to the Captain's Hat, which then moves on. One calm cutscene, a caption in the destination's dialect, then play.
      The rocket port and the tile dropThe black truck takes the crew to Launch, where the pink rocket tile falls from the sky and the ship drops after it (Moon Mission's opening). That is Option B used once, as a reveal. From then on the rocket on its port is Option A. The map is C, unlocked by Daddy's hideaway.
      Kid-simpleThree rules: tap the way, go the way you came, hold a coin to remember. No reading, no list of places, no setting, no "select world".
      Winchester, the final region

      Seven hexes, one greater hex, three regions of about a third

      A centre hex and its six neighbours form one mega-structure. The centre is the church park and common. Each region takes two outer hexes and one wedge of the centre; the common's own disk is always Downtown. Launch is off the map to the north, New Nova to the south, and the three regions are walked, never changed like worlds: crossing an edge changes the light, the music and a painted sign, nothing else.

      Region ids come from simulations/abbietown/regions.mjs (downtown, anis-side, abbie-daddy-side); the older names School Hill, Pond Side and Lake Country are sub-areas. Shares are measured on the real 760 m hexes by sampling the greater hex. No real addresses appear: homes are placed loosely, as the town study says.

      Plan

      What to build, in order

      1. Boards library. simulations/worlds/boards.mjs sits on the existing board store: list the saved board ids, move the current pointer, new / copy / rename / attic. No change to how a single board saves.
      2. Zoom out in the sandbox. Past the region edge the camera pulls back to the board of boards, with painted signs and the dashed new-board hex. Keep the ◎ World menu Boards page as the grown-up route.
      3. Land as its own choice. Fold ?world=basin into a per-board land, so one set of tiles can stand on the Ranch floor or in the Basin.
      4. Then travel. The model for doors and rides is worlds.mjs; the black truck is the first ride.
      5. Land the travel model. simulations/worlds/worlds.mjs (gates, first-arrival rewards, crew, vote) with its test. Done in this study; the Moon Mission thread can read the same file for Main Street, Launch and Moon base.
      6. Make the backpack travel. Items and the visited list join coins and charms in the account-backed store, so a second page of the game sees the same backpack.
      7. One ride first: the black truck. Café curb to Launch, with the cutscene, the rocket tile drop and the crew's job presses. Everything else copies it.
      8. Doors next. Cellar bulkhead (needs the key), Muraco door, lab door: each pairs two existing pages with the camera-through move.
      9. The train and the ranch gate. New Nova and the Ranch, then the map page as a reward from Daddy's hideaway.
      10. Decisions to confirm with Evan. The truck is the first ride (recommended); the rocket tile drop is a one-time reveal (recommended); the map arrives as a reward, not at the start (recommended).
