How to review

Each card says what makes the light, how studios build it (from talks, breakdowns and the Oracle's notes on the games we own), what our live version does, and where it would live in Abbie's World. The rank is my recommendation. Every look is only numbers: a sun, a sky, a ground bounce, fog, a few warm lamps, a rim, a shadow softness and a colour grade. That is why they stay fast and why one game signal can drive any of them.

Performance bar: the tile-placement standard. One shadow map, three point lights at most (no shadows), one fresnel rim in the material, fog and the grade are free (a CSS filter), no post-processing pass, and nothing is rebuilt when the light changes. Cards only draw while you can see them.

What I'd build first

The first three change how the world feels to a child, not just how it looks: soft light that never goes black, one clock that drives everything, and the long golden hour that tells you the day is ending. The Halloween look is last on the list but the most fun: orange pools of light on a purple dusk, never dark.

Where it connects
  • Look canon. docs/design/look/LOOK.md says every page sits under the world's own sky at its time of day, and glass is a lens that shows that sky. These looks are the sky: the page background, the glass and the world read the same signal.
  • Shader threads. Painted Brush, Watercolour and Paint-the-world (AW-STUDY-0045) decide how a surface answers light. This study decides the light that arrives. They multiply: Painted Brush wants a warm key and a teal rim; Watercolour wants high, flat, low-contrast light; Paint-the-world wants the hard, saturated beat rig. Each look here lists its match.
  • Nothing masks the world. No vignette, no edge haze, no backdrop blur. Fog is in the world and thins toward the player; the grade is colour only.
Design card

What the player feels or knows: "It's morning, it's getting late, the café is warm, the Moon is cold and hard, and it's Halloween."

How would the character know? The light is the world: long shadows, warm windows, lamps that come on, a hard white sun with no air on the Moon. Nobody reads a number.

Mode: none and diegetic. Light is how the world is, never an interface. The grade and the time slider here are grown-up tools; players only ever see the day.

Who it's for: the player (the child). The pick board is a grown-up tool.

The whole game adapts: one clock value picks a point on the day, and sky, sun, shadow tint, fog, lamps, window glow, music and animals all read it. Place dialects change the keys, not the grammar: the Ranch is wood and bells, the café is warm window light, the Moon base is hard sun and cold fill with lamp-lit rooms.

Halloween: playful-tense, never scary. A violet dusk, orange pools of light, low friendly fog. The floor of the exposure is high enough that nothing hides.

Style, never expression: every scene is built from our own primitives and models. No game screenshots, textures, shaders or code were used. Sources are public talks and articles, linked on each card.
