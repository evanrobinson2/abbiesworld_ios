What makes a great card

Ten card games and the premium print trade, boiled down to a spec and a rubric for Abbie's World cards. Every card on this page is drawn for this study; none copies another game's art, frames or text. Touch a card to tilt it, then deal the pack.

Each face-down card glows its rarity before it turns. Seed turns at once; Sun waits 1.2 seconds with a pulse. That wait is the anticipation Hearthstone's pack glow and a pack tear create.

The short version

Eight things every great card does

It is an object firstThickness, edge, material and moving light. A flat rectangle reads as a website.
The art is the card50 to 65% of a standard face, all of a top-tier one. The frame is a stage.
It has levels of detailInspect shows everything, the hand shows art and numbers, the board shows art, cost and one stat.
A cost and two stats, at mostBig, outlined, in fixed corners. Everything else is an icon or waits for inspect.
Rarity is triple-codedColour, shape and finish together, so it survives colour blindness and a 72 px thumbnail.
Rarity shows before the flipA glow and a wait that grow with rarity. Half the joy is the anticipation.
Top tiers change the silhouetteBorderless, frame-break, living art and foil, recognisable across the room.
Collectible down to the metadataSet mark, number out of total, maker line and variant code. Kids complete sets.
The games

What each one teaches

Schematics are abstract layout diagrams, drawn here, showing where the elements sit. Gold is a number, the light block is the art window, the lined block is text.

Readability

One card, three sizes

Every digital card game draws a different card at each size. Rules text only has to be readable when you inspect a card; in the hand you need art, name and numbers; on the board, art, cost and one stat. The checker's thumbnail test shrinks a card to 72 px tall and asks a vision model for cost, stat, rarity and type.

The Abbie's World card

Anatomy

CostTop-left token. One chunky outlined numeral, 18 pt or more in print.
NameRibbon across the top. Rounded display face, 10 pt or more, 18 characters or fewer.
Art windowIts outline is the card type. 50 to 65% of the face. One subject, centred, with headroom.
Rarity gemOn the seam between art and text plate. A growth stage: seed, sprout, bloom, harvest, sun.
Text plateNever text on raw art. 15 words or fewer, or up to three ability icons. 8 pt or more.
StatsBottom-left and bottom-right tokens. Two stats at most; the board view keeps one.
Collector lineSet mark, number out of total, maker line (Midjourney job or artist), variant code. Inside the 3 mm safe area.
FrameRanch materials: pine, paint, brass, lacquer, gilt. 15 to 30% of the face, with a real bevel.
Type by shape

The window's outline says what the card is

Hearthstone and Slay the Spire both arrived at this: you know the card type before you read a word. Our shapes come from things on the Ranch, so they don't echo either game.

Rarity

Growth stages, from Seed to Sun

Kids already know that a seed becomes a sprout, a bloom and a harvest. Each tier changes the gem's shape, its colour and lightness, and the frame's material. The finish (Shimmer foil, Golden Hour, Frost) is a separate axis that any tier can carry.

Print

Collection grade on paper

Trim63 × 88 mm, the standard trading-card size. Fits 66 × 91 mm sleeves.
Bleed3 mm beyond trim on every side. Art and frame colour run into it.
SafeText, numbers and gems stay 3 mm inside trim and clear of the 3 mm corner arcs.
PixelsMaster at 600 ppi: 1630 × 2221 px with bleed (trim 1488 × 2079). 300 ppi is the floor.
ColourPDF/X-4, coated CMYK (FOGRA39 or GRACoL 2013). Total ink 300% or less. Small text in 100% black only.
Stock300 to 330 gsm black-core card, about 0.30 mm, smooth or linen.
FinishesHolofoil laminate, cold foil, spot UV, etched foil, embossing, a holo stamp on rare and up. Each needs a mask layer.

The rubric

Seven gates, then 100 points

Any failed gate rejects the card. A card ships at 80 points or more. The full rubric, with thresholds and how each check is measured, is docs/oracle/cards/card-rubric.json; the reference checker is tools/card-rubric/check_card.py.

GatePasses when
GeometryCanvas is 1630 × 2221 px at 600 ppi, or 814 × 1109 at 300 ppi, with bleed
Safe areaEvery text, number and gem box is 3 mm inside trim and clear of the corner arcs
Text sizeText 8 pt, name 10 pt, numerals 18 pt or more in print
ContrastText 4.5:1 against its plate; numerals 3:1
Art resolutionArt source at least the window's size at 300 ppi, upscaled 2× at most
Expression ledgerNo other game's art, frame, icon, font or text; provenance recorded
Kid-safePG; reading grade 3 or below; 15 words or fewer on the face
The legal line

Style we learn, expression we never copy

Free to learn from

  • Cost in a corner, stats in the bottom corners, rarity tiers, foil: card-game conventions
  • Shape-coded card types and rarity symbols as ideas
  • Levels of detail, two-number faces, text on inspect
  • Variant ladders, finishes and flares as collectible axes
  • Juice timings, tilt, glow before the reveal
  • All print specs and finishes

Never copied

  • Any game's card art, frame art, gems, symbols, card backs
  • Their fonts (Beleren, Plantin and the rest) and logos
  • Characters, creatures and card names or text
  • One game's whole look taken together (trade dress)
  • Another game's card image as a Midjourney or modeler reference

Every game's findings are recorded as identity-schema signatures, each marked style or expression, in docs/oracle/cards/card-signatures.json.
