Game Oracle · human characters in 3D

How 3D games build their people

The Oracle has 379 games. These 30 are 3D and have human characters you see clearly. They are sorted on a ladder from toy-like to realistic, with what each does with proportions, faces, hands and detail. These are written observations from the Oracle's study, not copied art.

What the Oracle has learned so far
Pick one rung and stay on itEvery game that reads well commits to one level of stylisation for heads, hands, clothes and props. Mixing a realistic face with a toy body is what looks wrong.
Heads get bigger as games get friendlierToy and cosy games sit at 2 to 4 heads tall; heroic stylised games at 6 to 7; realistic games at 7.5 to 8.
Silhouette before detailTeam Fortress 2, Marvel Rivals and Fall Guys prove a character must read as a shape from far away before any texture counts.
Faces: few features, big expressionsStylised games keep eyes, brows and mouth large and simple, and drop pores, wrinkles and fine noses. The nose stays, as a simple shape.
Hands are simplified, never removedEven LEGO keeps a readable hand. Stylised games use chunky mitten-like hands with a separate thumb, sometimes four fingers.
Clothes carry the detailStylised humans move detail into big readable clothing shapes (a hat, a scarf, a belt) instead of small surface noise.
Flat or soft colour, little textureMost friendly 3D humans use flat or gently painted colour with a few material roles (skin, cloth, leather, metal), which suits our engineered-geometry modeler.
Our rungFor Abbie's World: 4 to 6 heads tall, real adult proportions in limbs, simple faces with nose and brows, mitten hands with a thumb, chunky clothes. Between Dreamlight Valley and Valheim.

Sources: the Oracle's study profiles (docs/oracle/games) and its public-knowledge notes on each game's character style. Head counts are estimates. Each game name opens its Oracle page.
