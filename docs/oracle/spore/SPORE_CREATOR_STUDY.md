# Spore: creator, evolution step and loop (study for Abbie's science adventure)

Style yes, expression never. Everything here is in our own words from public writing (links at the end). No game files,
frames or recordings are used. A private recording from Evan's PC will replace the estimates when it lands.

## 1. The big loop, from slide to escape
Spore is one long loop of **play, earn, edit, play again**, repeated at larger and larger scales.
1. Arrive: a meteor lands, a first cell is born (auto-generated). Pick a diet mouth.
2. Play: swim, eat what your mouth likes, flee what is bigger, ram meteor blocks, beat cells that carry parts.
3. Earn: DNA fills a bar. Each of ten size steps needs about five foods, so a reward lands about every minute.
4. Summon the editor: press Call Mate, follow the sound, swim into the mate. Editing is a place in the world.
5. Edit: spend DNA on parts, place, scale, paint. Changes show live. Leave and you hatch as the new design.
6. Grow: your old peers turn into prey. The next size class drifts blurred in the layer below.
7. Escape: see the sea floor, fill the bar, a timeline of your choices decides your diet, you leave the water.
Later stages repeat the same shape (creature: parts from fossils, brain size, social or predator path, nest via mating
call; and onward to tribe, city, civilisation, space). The same editor idea returns each time.

## 2. The creator (cell editor), what makes it good
- **Parts panel on the left, stats top right, your cell big in the middle.** One mode for building, one for paint.
- **Must-have mouth.** Eyes and propulsion are optional; no eyes means a smaller view of the pool.
- **Caps per part** (1 spike, up to 6 poison, up to 6 electric, up to 5 speed parts) force real choices.
- **A complexity meter** goes red when the cell is too much: the budget is visible, never a lecture.
- **Placement matters:** a spike only protects where it sits. The editor is a decision, not decoration.
- **Paint never changes performance.** Pattern plus colour is pure expression, so kids can't do it "wrong".
- **Symmetry and a spine of pullable points** (creature editor) make odd bodies easy to shape.
- **Soft mastery (Gingold):** "disproportionate feedback", few modes, constraints that make things easier, fuzzy
  hidden judgment of what looks good, "make something cool in three clicks".
- **Rigblocks:** every part is a pre-rigged block with named clips ("open and close mouth", "grasp"). One authored clip
  plays across every part with that name. Skin paint blends with hand-painted masks and three tint colours, so artists
  keep control and player choices still show.
- **Animate by meaning:** retarget the same motion to any body the child builds (see layers.animation in the record).
- **Players will find uses you did not plan** (a mouth used as a flower). Decide early which to keep.

## 3. Cell stage HUD (what is on screen)
Bottom: a progress bar of DNA toward the next step, which becomes the advance button. Bottom-right: your cell and health;
a small bar appears over anything that is hit (green left, red last hit, black lost). No map. Size is the threat readout:
prey, peer, predator, epic. See `layers.information_grammar` in the record.

## 4. What this means for Abbie's science adventure (recommendations)
1. **Keep the mate-call trick, in our fiction:** a glowing friend-blob sings; follow the sound; the lab-bench editor opens
   as a zoom into the cell. No menu.
2. **Editor in four moves:** pick a body, drag parts, scale, paint. Three paints and three tints, never wrong.
3. **Ten growth steps, five foods each,** with a golden reward animation each step (reuse the coin reward).
4. **Visible budget, not a number:** a wobble meter ("too busy") instead of DNA arithmetic on screen.
5. **Parts that work only where placed,** each with a sound and a world effect (poison cloud, electric crackle).
6. **Depth by blur layers** (our depth of field plus SVG cell rims), moving the player up a layer as they grow.
7. **Escape as a ceremony:** a timeline of what the child did becomes their creature's "diet", then the legs-or-wings moment
   leaves the water. Our version: the beaker's green goo becomes the doorway to the next mini-game.
8. **Share what you made:** a creature is tiny data (about 4 KB in the original). Ours can be a few numbers too.
