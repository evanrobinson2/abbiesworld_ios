Ranch and Moon remaster

"Redo the crop bed grass, goat thing looks weird out of place. Lamps need upgrades. Dispatch a moon remastering. No holds barred given all our improvements."

Every asset and effect in the Ranch tile, ranked by how much it hurts the look, and a plan to rebuild the Moonbase from the Moon Mission pack. Each item names its tier, its concept-art need and its owner thread. Before shots come from fixed cameras that the after shots will reuse.

The headline finding

Everything Evan flagged is the second wave the rebuild never reached.

The Ranch rebuild replaced the 35 file-based models. Its brief set aside a second wave of things built in code: the sugar shack, the playground, fences and gates, lamp posts, garden beds and the residents. The crop bed, the grass, the lamps and the fences are that wave: boxes, cones and cylinders standing beside rebuilt models, so they look like placeholders, because they are.

The goat shelter is a different break. It is a good rebuilt model (pink walls, teal roof, from Evan's own pick), but the open-shed pass strips its walls, leaving a floating roof on salmon posts with grey slabs underneath. Pink appears nowhere else in the Ranch.

No holds barred means applying everything at once: the aesthetic study's palette and one-signal light, the HUD study's "show it in the world", the card study's "the tile must look like its card", the four granularity tiers (hero, kit, grammar, impostor), smooth shading, the parts-first rule, the Midjourney-to-model pipeline with the Review Desk, the footprint rule and the game-feel check.

Ranch audit

Fourteen things, worst first

Hurt is screen share times how wrong it reads, out of five. Owners: the Model challenge thread for models and code-built props; the Weather and clouds thread for sky, sun, night and lamp light.

R1

Grass and ground cover

Today. Hundreds of hard green cones and pyramids on flat, over-saturated green. It is the biggest share of the tile, and the flat-shaded spikes read as a bed of nails. The meadow is louder than the buildings.

Remaster. Six soft clump shapes with round-tipped blades and smooth normals, instanced, colour drifting from meadow to foliage, swaying with the weather's wind, thinned near paths and buildings. Clover and flower patches come from grammar. The ground drops one step in chroma so the toys stand out.

Crop bed

Today. A 5×5 grid of flat green cubes on a brown slab, right beside the home. The most toy-like spot on the tile is a placeholder.

Remaster. A raised timber bed in the language of the planter already beside it: mounded soil rows, stakes and twine, a watering can. Crops come from a kit in three stages (sprout, leafy, ripe). Ripe crops glow gold; thirsty ones go brown and droop, so the bed tells you what it needs.

Moon

The Moonbase today, and the world in the pack

The Moon tile is a believable NASA-style colony: white inflatable rings and lobes, a ferry port, robots and white astronauts on beige regolith. The Moon Mission pack is a different and better world, and the Moonbase card already shows its painting. Today the card promises a place the tile does not have.

What hurts today

M1

Identity

The tile matches neither its card nor its story: no dome, no tower, no Abbie, Daddy, buggy, meteorite or Star Child.

M2

Regolith

Beige sand-plaster, flat with crease lines. No craters, though crater fields are the pack's defining landscape.

M3

Rocks

A uniform confetti of dark faceted dodecahedra across the whole tile.

M4

Light

The Moon's dialect is lights, radio and holograms, yet nothing glows: no windows, beacons or crystals. Measured at 10 pm, the tile is exactly as bright as at noon.

M5

Gold bars

Utility-trench markers read as floating gold bricks in dashed lines, like a developer overlay.

M6

People

Generic white astronauts.

M7

Landing pad

A big grey concrete U with stairs, the heaviest shape on the tile.

M8

Slab edge

Black void sides.

Priority

Order of work

  1. Ranch wave 1, what Evan sees first: grass, crop bed, shelters, lamps.
  2. Light on both tiles in one pass: Ranch sky and night with the Moon's Earthshine, windows and beacons. One signal, one palette; night darker than day.
  3. Moon wave 1: crater terrain, buggy, meteorite, then the dome.
  4. Ranch wave 2: fences, smooth foliage, paths, slab edge, water, yards.
  5. Moon wave 2: Star Child, Abbie and Daddy, habitat kit, horizon, cleanup.
  6. Wave 3: sugar shack, playground, animals, light cycle.

Done means

  • After shots from the same script and cameras, side by side with these.
  • Every model through the Review Desk; concept art to Evan as soon as it lands.
  • The aesthetic checker at 80 or more at noon, dusk and night (today: 36), with night measurably darker than day.
  • The game-feel check passes, and new models keep their old footprints.
