#LookWhy for Abbie's WorldWhereCost
Design card

What the player feels or knows: "My world looks like a picture book, and it changes with the day, the music and the weather."

How would the character know? The light, wind, glow and colour are the world itself: wind bends the grass toward where you're going, lamps glow at dusk, the café floor pulses with the song.

Mode: none and diegetic. Shading is how the world is, so it never becomes interface. Outlines and the lens look stay on the presentation side and characters don't perceive them.

Who it's for: the player (the child). The pick board here is a grown-up tool.

The whole game adapts: every look is one material whose numbers (sun, shadow tint, rim, fog, glow, wind) come from one game signal, so time, weather, mood and music move light, sound and animals together.

Style, never expression: every scene is built from our own models and primitives. No game screenshots, textures, shaders or code were used. Sources are public talks and articles, linked on each card.
