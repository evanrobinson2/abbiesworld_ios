Winchester in seven hexes

Seven hexes, three zones. Each hex is one hand-modelled miniature with a single job, drawn on the real hills, ponds and roads. Nothing gets built until you approve this sheet.

The master plan

/*MASTER*/
DowntownAni's sideAbbie and Daddy's sideRailwayLaunch RoadReal water

Drawn from public MassGIS data at true scale: 10 m contours, lakes, brooks, roads and the railway. North is up. The dashed spokes in the centre hex are the 120° cuts that make the three zones one third each on the ground. Homes are placed loosely, with no addresses.

Three zones, seven hexes

Downtown

H1 Square, H2 Depot and River, H3 Reservoir Hills

The place where things happen: the town square, the coffee shop and its basement, Frozen Hoagies, the lab, the train, the big water. Three hexes, because it holds the most doors.

Ani's side

H4 Neighbourhood Streets, H5 Ani's Pond Side

Ani's cul de sac and tree house, Horn Pond with its splash pad and hill, and Launch on the ridge. A quiet suburb you can wander, with the nine house types on show.

Abbie and Daddy's side

H6 Abbie's Yard, H7 Gingerbread Hill

Abbie's house, back yard and tree house, the school door, then a road to the Gingerbread House, where Daddy's sealed bunker door waits in the hillside.

Three things to confirm

1. Hex count by zone. I recommend Downtown 3, Ani 2, Abbie 2, because the centre hex is the square. On the ground the zones still carve one third each. The other option is to split the centre hex three ways, which makes the square three small, cramped pieces.

2. Launch Road. The car road runs from Launch in H5 across the north of H6 to Gingerbread Hill in H7, so Launch sits some distance from both homes, as you asked.

3. Ginn Field. It shrinks to a small train-park corner in H2, not a hero, since you asked for none.
