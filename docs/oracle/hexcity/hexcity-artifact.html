A founding city on hexes

A new place for Abbie's World, found the way people found places: a limestone headland, a river, a sheltered bay and a spring high on the ridge. Then today's big tile splits into 217 small hexes, each a city block, and a town grows on them from the land up. Drag the town, play the years, zone a block and watch the town answer. Every building above was placed by the simulation when a need was measured, and the log says why.

Anno 117: Pax Romana is in the Game Oracle (installed and studied), but only as a light profile: its loop, its information mix and its Game DNA. This study adds the deeper Anno identity record the Oracle was missing, and borrows Anno's mechanics, never its look or words.

The founding place

The land chose it

The headland is not drawn. A ridge is raised, rain runs off and cuts channels, rain that soaks into the limestone comes out as a spring where the rock meets the valley clay, and the sea fills the low ground. The founders land in the calmest water and walk the land, costing every place in minutes of daily walking, then turn down the places that flood, sit below the storm line or cannot see the bay.

      How the town works

      Nine systems, each from first principles

      Anno's lesson is that a city is chains: a need, the goods that answer it, the land and the hands that make the goods. SimCity's is that the street is where it all meets. Here each block is a hex, and each system is a rule about the physical world, not a number to tune.

      What this needs to become a game

      • Dress the blocks with the kit's real low-poly models (houses by kind, the works, the channel's arches) instead of the blocks and boxes here.
      • Put the headland into the world map as its own region, with the founding tile at its heart and the countryside tiles around it as the town's fields, groves, flocks and woods.
      • Design the town cast's voices (steward, baker, fisher, builder, water carrier) in the family-cast voice pipeline, then pick them on the cast page.
      • Let the child place the works too (Anno's way), with the council suggesting where by the same measured needs.
      • Add the music per kind of household, and seasons: olive harvest, wine pressing, winter storms closing the bay.
