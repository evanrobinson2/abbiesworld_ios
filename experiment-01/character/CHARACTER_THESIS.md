# Character Thesis: Pip Starling

## Identity

**Name:** Pip Starling  
**Age:** 8-10 years old  
**Role:** The Curious Explorer

## Personality Profile

**Core Trait:** Insatiable curiosity — asks "why?" and "what if?" constantly

**Contradiction:** Despite boundless curiosity about the world, Pip is quietly uncertain about their own abilities. They don't see themselves as brave, even when they are.

**Motivation:** To understand how things work and help creatures encountered along the way

**Attitude Toward Danger:** Wide-eyed fascination first, sensible caution second

**Movement Personality:** Light, quick, bouncy. Center of gravity shifts forward when interested. Arms often slightly raised or gesturing. Never completely still.

## Visual Design Principles

**Dominant Shape Language:** Rounded, soft shapes (circles, ovals) suggesting approachability and youth

**Proportions:**
- Head: ~1/3 of total height (larger than realistic)
- Torso: Short, thin
- Limbs: Long and thin (animated proportions)
- Hands/Feet: Small

**Signature Silhouette Feature:** Windswept hair with 2-3 distinct flowing tendrils that suggest perpetual motion

**Costume Concept:** 
- Simple explorer's outfit
- Loose comfortable shirt (warm ochre/mustard)
- Practical pants/shorts (soft teal/muted green)
- Sturdy boots
- Small satchel for collected treasures

**Palette:**
- Primary: Warm ochre/mustard yellow
- Secondary: Soft teal or muted green  
- Accent: Warm coral/terracotta (hair)
- Skin: Warm medium tone

## Three Descriptive Adjectives

**Buoyant** — **Attentive** — **Windswept**

## Design Selection Rationale

After exploring 10 silhouette variations across two exploration rounds, the chosen direction combines:
- Lanky animated proportions from exploration #3 (clear limb separation)
- Windswept hair personality from exploration #5/#4 (movement suggestion)

The windswept flowing hair was selected because it gives the character personality before any detail is added — you can sense motion and curiosity from the silhouette alone.

**Risk Noted:** Hair complexity may cause drift across generations. Mitigation: Simplify to 2-3 distinct tendrils with clear geometric relationship.
